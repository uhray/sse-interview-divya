# Exercise 3: System design, the job feed importer

No code for this one. It's a whiteboard conversation. There's no single right answer,
and we'll push on whatever you propose.

## Background

We run job boards for trucking companies. Most clients don't type their jobs into our
system. They give us a URL to an XML feed, generated by their applicant tracking system
or by an ad agency, and we import it.

**Today's importer:**

- Runs **hourly**. It downloads every active client's feed (about **90 clients**, from a
  handful of postings up to about 2,000), parses it and reconciles it against the jobs we
  already have.
- There are about **40 feed formats**. Each one has a hand-written **handler**: code that
  knows where the postings live in that XML and how to map its fields (title, pay, route
  type, hiring area, experience required, and so on) onto our job schema. Adding a new
  format takes an engineer a day or two, plus registering it in several places.
- Field mapping is **string matching**: "owner operator", "Owner-Operator" and
  "owner_operator" need separate handling, and "2 - 3 years" experience gets parsed by a
  regex.
- Postings are **rolled up into jobs**. One client might post the same role 300 times
  (once per city). We group them into one job using a **rollup key** chosen per client
  (title, campaign name, a URL slug...). A job's identity is that key.
- **Reconciliation:** postings with a key we haven't seen become new jobs. Keys that
  vanished from the feed get archived. If certain fields change (title, description,
  apply URL), the job goes back to **pending** and a person in Ops must re-approve it
  before it shows again.
- Ops has a per-client **"Manual Mode"** switch that freezes all imports for that client
  until someone runs one by hand.

**What goes wrong:**

1. A provider changes something upstream (collapses every campaign name to the same
   value, reorders the feed, adds a tracking pixel with a rotating token to every
   description). Rollup keys or content hashes change, so dozens of jobs get archived or
   pushed to pending overnight. We usually find out when a client calls.
2. Ops spends hours a week re-approving jobs that didn't meaningfully change.
3. Onboarding a new feed format is slow and engineer-only.
4. Mapping mistakes (wrong route type, missed hiring areas) are silent. The job just
   performs badly.

## The ask

Leadership wants to "rebuild the importer around AI." You've been asked to lead it.

- What would you build? Walk us through the architecture.
- Where does an LLM actually help, and where should it stay out?
- How do you keep it from doing something dumb to 2,000 jobs at 3am?
- How do you know it's working, and what does it cost?
- How do you get from today's system to yours without a big-bang cutover?
